# THE LAST WARD
## COMPLETE GAME DESIGN & DEVELOPMENT MASTER LIST

**Project:** The Last Ward  
**Genre:** Psychological survival horror / exploration / puzzles / resource management / combat  
**Setting:** Blackridge Medical Complex, 1998  
**Corporation:** Monolith Pharmaceuticals  
**Corporate Motto:** "Preserving Life. Securing Peace."  
**Secret Research Project:** LULLABY  
**Main Pursuer:** Subject X  
**Primary Development Focus:** Chapter 1 — The First Floor

---

# 1. THE MAIN VISION

- Create a survival-horror game inspired by the atmosphere, exploration, and tension of classic Resident Evil, Resident Evil Survivor, Silent Hill, and Outlast.
- The game must have its own original identity, lore, locations, characters, and enemies.
- Exploration must be the primary gameplay activity.
- Puzzles, resource management, combat, psychological horror, and survival should support exploration.
- The game must not feel like a simple sequence of collecting items and moving between rooms.
- Every meaningful discovery should contribute to survival, progression, storytelling, or atmosphere.
- Players should want to inspect the environment because they might discover something useful, disturbing, or important.
- Horror should come from uncertainty, atmosphere, consequences, and occasional dangerous encounters.
- Avoid constant jump scares and endless combat.
- Give players quiet moments between major incidents.
- Backtracking must be meaningful.
- Aim for approximately 2–3 hours of exploratory gameplay in Chapter 1, subject to actual playtesting.
- Finish Chapter 1 properly before expanding Chapter 2.

---

# 2. STORY FOUNDATION

- The game takes place in Blackridge Medical Complex in 1998.
- Blackridge is connected to Monolith Pharmaceuticals.
- Monolith presents itself as a respectable medical corporation.
- Its official motto is **"Preserving Life. Securing Peace."**
- Monolith secretly conducts experiments through Project LULLABY.
- LULLABY involves memory, consciousness, identity, and attempts to reconstruct or preserve human experience.
- The hospital has suffered a catastrophic containment failure.
- Infection and experimentation-related abnormalities have spread through the facility.
- Monolith initiated a security lockdown to prevent the situation from escaping.
- Containment has already failed inside the hospital.
- The lockdown traps employees, patients, survivors, and the protagonist inside.
- Monolith leadership knowingly prioritizes secrecy and containment over rescuing survivors.
- The corporation's responsibility should be discovered gradually through files, recordings, photographs, and physical evidence.
- The protagonist initially has no idea who he is.
- His connection to LULLABY must remain mysterious until late Chapter 1.
- Bela is connected to LULLABY and must have an important role in the larger mystery.
- Bela is not a morality judge who evaluates the player's decisions.
- Chapter 1 ends with a major revelation concerning the protagonist.
- The revelation should answer one major question while creating another mystery.
- The underground laboratory becomes the setting of Chapter 2.

# 7. INTERACTION SYSTEM

**Main rule: ONE VISIBLE OBJECT = ONE LOGICAL INTERACTION.**

- Click the actual depicted object to interact with it.
- Avoid generic lists containing unrelated objects.
- No payphone that also opens window interactions.
- No directory that contains unrelated radios and barricades.
- No giant visible interaction masks.
- Invisible hitboxes must align with visible objects.
- Door and object hitboxes must not overlap.
- Hotspots must not sit on empty walls.
- Important objects should have subtle discoverability hints.
- Use gentle glints or highlights where appropriate.
- Hovering may reveal a subtle outline or label.
- Avoid forcing the player to blindly sweep the mouse across every room.
- Do not make every object glow constantly.
- Mandatory key items must be reasonably visible or signposted.
- Optional secrets may be less obvious.
- Simple inspections can produce short narration without opening a new interface.
- Important containers and puzzles use close-up inspection views.
- Closing a close-up returns to the exact room.
- Inspections should happen within the central scene.
- Opening a container should reveal only its actual contents.
- Desks usually contain a small number of relevant objects.
- Safes usually contain one significant reward.
- Files should not consume inventory slots.
- Items must be collected individually.
- Full inventory means the object remains where it was found.
- Opened drawers and collected objects should preserve their states.

# 10. ONLINE AND OFFLINE GRAPHICS

One game with two artwork systems.

**OFFLINE**
- All core code is available locally.
- All required room artwork is bundled.
- Item graphics are bundled.
- Required sound effects are bundled.
- Combat, puzzles, inventory, and story work offline.
- Saving and loading work without internet.
- The player can complete the game offline.

**ONLINE AI-ENHANCED**
- The game can connect to an image-generation backend.
- A backend may generate atmospheric variations of rooms and corridors.
- Room identity and functional geometry must remain consistent.
- AI artwork must not remove or reposition required doors.
- AI artwork must not hide essential puzzle clues.
- AI artwork must not create misleading interactive objects.
- Generated variations may reflect the seed, sanity, lighting, and story progression.
- Successful online artwork should be cached.
- Returning to a room should not generate inconsistent new geometry.

**CONNECTION LOSS**
- The player can continue playing immediately.
- Cached or bundled artwork remains available.
- Progress and saves are unaffected.
- Gameplay never waits for image generation.
- Offline fallback happens automatically.

**STARTUP GRAPHICS SETTINGS**
- Automatic.
- Online AI-enhanced.
- Offline.

Important current limitation: the artwork backend adapter exists, but a real image-generation service is not already configured or live. The offline game is the reliable playable version.

**CAUTION**

**DANGER**

# 50. OVERALL DESIGN GOAL

The Last Ward should feel like an atmospheric survival-horror game where the player is trapped inside Blackridge Medical Complex.

The outside doors are chained and bolted.

The hospital is dark.

The protagonist doesn't know who he is.

Monolith Pharmaceuticals claims to preserve life, but its research has condemned patients and employees.

Every room might contain a useful object, a disturbing event, a dangerous creature, or another clue about Project LULLABY.

The player must explore, manage limited supplies, solve puzzles, decide whom to trust, and survive increasingly unsettling experiences.

Sometimes the threat is real.

Sometimes the protagonist's perception is unreliable.

Sometimes a seemingly ordinary object becomes horrifying.

And as the player uncovers more of the truth, Subject X begins hunting them through the hospital.

**The goal is not simply to reach the elevator.**

**The goal is to survive Blackridge, uncover what happened, and discover why the hospital remembers the protagonist even though he remembers nothing.**
